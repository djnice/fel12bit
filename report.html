P7 FEL 12-bit Gray Test

Does the 12-bit detail of the enhancement layer (the two lowest bits) reach the output? Measured on the Oppo, the S922X and the S928X (CoreELEC FEL composition) against a known 12-bit pattern.

Two capture rounds: Oppo and S922X FEL/MEL, then S922X again (bit-identical to the first) and the S928X with and without dither. Test file: P7_FEL_12bit_gray_test.mkv (v2). Every pixel is an exact integer, VDR12 = 4 × BL + (EL − 512). Band A is a 12-bit ramp of 60 bars one code apart (VDR12 1600…1659). Band B is the same BL without EL (15 steps of 4 codes). Band C holds EL blocks of ±16…±255. Band D is the ramp of band A in the dark (400…459).

The Oppo and the S922X output a 12-bit IPT tunnel, and the FEL's lowest bits reach it. On both, FEL minus MEL is exactly the EL contribution, and with MEL band A collapses to the 15 steps of band B. The S928X sends the same 12-bit tunnel through the same DV core curve as the S922X. It composes the EL correctly at 12 bit, but hands 10-bit frames to the DV core: without dither the extra bits are rounded away, with dither (the default) they arrive as the average of a fine fixed pattern.

For gray, the IPT I channel is the PQ code itself, so the exact unrounded reference is known for every bar: I = (VDR12/4096 − 0.0625) × 1.1687 × 4095. Dashed line: the row's mean offset. Thin lines: ±0.5 code around it, the band that rounding a 12-bit value can use.

Why the lowest bits don't match the reference

Each device has its own fixed-point PQ/IPT implementation, so its last bit is not expected to equal a double-precision rounding. What shows the bit depth is the spread that is left after removing a constant offset. An ideal 12-bit output can't get below 0.29 codes (1/√12). A 10-bit path, with 4.67 I codes per step, gives 1.35.

  • The Oppo keeps 12 bits. Its spread (0.31) is the minimum a 12-bit output can have. It mostly truncates instead of rounding: 87 % of the bars in band A equal floor(exact). The others sit near a fraction of .0 or .5, where any tiny difference in the PQ curve flips the last bit. Its curve also sits about 0.5 code low in band A and 2.3 codes low in the dark band D. A constant offset moves every level together and costs no resolution: all 60 levels stay distinct and one code apart.
  • The S922X has twice the 12-bit spread. This is not random noise: the same input always gives the same code, and the second-round capture is bit-identical to the first. It comes from a ±1 code wobble in the Amlogic DV core's curve, which already shows in band B, where there is no EL. Where the wobble dips, two neighbouring codes merge: bars 25/26 and 56/57 in band A, and 6/7, 27/28 and 46/47 in band D.
  • Every 10-bit case measures a spread of about 1.35, as the step size predicts.

S928X: our composition with hardware EL decoding

CoreELEC dv-fel-s5-20261001. The EL is decoded by the S928X's dual-layer HEVC decoder and composed on the GPU (Mali-G57). Two measurements: an HDMI tunnel capture with and without dither, which shows what leaves the box, and a readback of the compositor's output on our box, converted to IPT with the reference chain, which shows the signal before any device curve.

  • The S928X sends the same 12-bit IPT tunnel as the S922X, through the same DV core curve. Band B, which has no EL, is bit-identical to the S922X capture, including its offset and wobble.
  • The EL is composed correctly at 12 bit, but the composed picture is 10 bit. The S5 video layer takes 10-bit frames. With dither off, each 12-bit value is rounded to the nearest 10-bit code (1600, 1601 → 400; 1602…1605 → 401), so band A has 16 levels and a 10-bit spread (1.44). This is half a step away from MEL, which drops the EL and shows the BL step.
  • With dither (the default), the 12-bit value reaches the HDMI output as an average. A fixed blue-noise pattern spreads each bar over two neighbouring 10-bit codes, and the DV core passes the proportions through unchanged. Bar 1601 (400.25 in 10-bit terms) arrives as 75 % I 1568 and 25 % I 1573; bar 1602 (400.5) as 50 % / 50 %. The bar means give 60 distinct, monotonic levels in bands A and D.
  • The pattern is spatial only: all frames of both HDMI captures are bit-identical.
  • On the box, hardware and software EL decoding give the same levels. 0.2 % of the pixels differ by one code, which comes from the lossy BL of the source.

Should the S928X dither be on?

Yes, for watching. It is the default. Turn it off only for measurements that need bit-exact codes.

  • Without dither, every four neighbouring 12-bit values become one 10-bit code. A gradient the FEL renders in 12-bit steps comes out in 10-bit steps (HDMI band A: 16 levels, spread 1.44, like MEL).
  • With dither, the average over a few pixels keeps the 12-bit value, and the DV core passes it through (HDMI band A: 60 levels as means, spread 0.46). The finer gradation of the FEL survives instead of being rounded away.
  • The cost is a fine, fixed grain of one 10-bit step: two neighbouring codes, 5 I codes apart, on a flat area. The pattern is spatial only and every captured frame was bit-identical, so it cannot flicker. A one-step grain should not be visible at normal viewing distances.

Note on the test file

The first version was only 0.8 MB for 30 s, because every frame is identical and the P/B frames are almost empty. It stopped after a few frames or seconds on the S922X and the S928X. That was not a decoder problem. For dual-layer DV the decoder runs in stream mode, and Kodi only starts taking pictures once the 15 MB video buffer is 90 % full. The whole file never filled it, so playback ended when the demuxer reached the end of the file. v2 appends cabac_zero_words to every BL picture (~15.6 Mbit/s). Decoders skip them, so the decoded pictures are bit-identical to v1, and it now plays to the end with hardware EL decoding.

Method: every capture fully decoded (Oppo 36/32 frames, S922X 18/26 and 11 frames, S928X 10/11 frames). Measured at bar centres: the middle half in x and y, which avoids the EL upsampling edges. Bar edges sit 1 px right of the reference on both devices, so there is no shift or scaling. Reference: ref_ipt with the file's own RPU (YCbCr→RGB, RGB→LMS with crosstalk, PQ, LMS→IPTPQc2), full range.
Tools: ref_ipt, ipt_compare, ipt_preview.
